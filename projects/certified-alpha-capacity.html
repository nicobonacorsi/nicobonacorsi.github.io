Abstract

Certified Alpha Capacity asks whether a trading signal can accumulate enough statistical evidence for reliable deployment before its economic value decays. The framework measures evidence and remaining opportunity on a common Kullback–Leibler information scale, producing a certification frontier, explicit multiple-testing search penalties, and an equilibrium channel through which arbitrage activity shortens the lifetime of certifiable opportunities.

AlphaValue is the open-source implementation: a Python package, command-line interface, public test suite, reproducibility materials, and an interactive Alpha Survival Frontier calculator.

Question

Can an alpha generate enough future information to meet a declared false-deployment and power target before the opportunity reaches its economic endpoint?

Core idea

Put statistical evidence and economic lifetime on the same information clock. In the canonical Gaussian experiment, reliable certification requires lifetime KL information to exceed an exact threshold:

\[ \mathcal I_{\mathrm{life}} \ge \mathcal I_{\mathrm{crit}}(\alpha,\beta) = \frac{1}{2} \left(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{1-\beta}\right)^2. \]

Under zero-hurdle exponential decay, lifetime information becomes a function of initial Sharpe and half-life. This produces the Alpha Survival Frontier and makes the statistical cost of search breadth explicit when many candidate signals are screened.

Main results

  • Exact Gaussian certification threshold and a general KL data-processing converse.
  • Alpha Survival Frontiers with explicit multiple-testing and heterogeneous error-budget effects.
  • An endogenous crowding equilibrium in which arbitrage entry reduces residual lifetime information.
  • A multivariate value-per-information bound linking statistical distinguishability to trading-cost curvature.

Reproducibility

The public AlphaValue release contains the reference Python implementation, CLI, synthetic examples, tests, and compact reproduction materials for the empirical calculations. The repository links the manuscript, live calculator, and versioned Zenodo archive under a stable concept DOI.
